For the first question, I want to tell you the story of why I came up with Chunked.

I love trying new routines and exploring different interests. There are many things I want to do throughout my day, week, and month. I wanted something that would help me stay organized and follow a plan while still feeling comfortable, free, and not exhausted.

I tried different planning methods and apps, including Notion, Kanban tools, Google Calendar, the Study Together website, and the Pomodoro technique. But I kept running into the same problems.

Sometimes, I spent too much time writing goals, planning everything, and setting up too many habits. Other times, I skipped my routines. Even when an app offered streak freezes or allowed me to skip a day, I felt lost when I wanted to return. Sometimes, I would delete everything and start from scratch.

Even on days when I accomplished things, looking at everything I hadn’t finished made me feel as though I had done nothing. And when an app showed too many features at once—especially when I wanted to focus and feel calm—I became overwhelmed. It gave me more things to think about when I needed fewer.

I also don’t want to feel punished for skipping something. Instead, I want help understanding why it happened. Was my plan unrealistic? Were my tasks poorly distributed throughout the day? Was my energy low? Could my personal preferences or personality play a role? I even wondered whether something like my MBTI type might help explain it.

Eventually, I went back to sticky notes. I divided my day into parts—sometimes around prayer times, sometimes into morning, afternoon, and evening. Then I wrote down what I wanted to do in each chunk.

I placed just one chunk in front of me and kept the others behind it.

That simple change helped me. I could focus on the chunk in front of me, finish things, notice my progress, and understand my energy better. It gave my different interests their own space in the day. When the next chunk started, I could make a fresh start instead of carrying the weight of everything I hadn’t finished in the previous one.

That’s where Chunked came from: having many interests, building a few habits, dividing the day in a way that fits me, and feeling comfortable while making progress.

The atmosphere matters to me, too—lighting a candle, playing background music, putting on an aesthetic video, or choosing a calming theme. I want planning to feel like a space that supports me, where I can focus, enjoy what I’m doing, and return without guilt when I lose my rhythm.
